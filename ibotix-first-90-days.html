The motion I would run

Founder-led today, repeatable by day 90, partner-led plus direct after that.

My framework: the path of least friction

Every deal has two hurdles: the friction of changing how people work, and the threshold to sign, meaning effort, risk, approvals and cost.

For the first 10 to 15 DocForensics customers in a sector, I go where both are lowest: customers who already trust you, accounts a partner already holds, your investors' network and yours. I check the path is real, land the first logos there, and then build the repeatable motion from what those deals teach us. The same rule works inside each deal: find the route with the fewest approvals and the clearest owner, or create one.

The other half is what I call the promised land. A head of operations buys when they can see their team after the change: reviewing exceptions instead of typing. To learn that picture fast, I sit with your delivery and customer success teams, because that is where it already lives.

From founder-led to repeatable

  1. We sell together first. You are on the early deals as the executive sponsor. Early enterprise deals are signed on the founder's word.
  2. I close some deals too, to learn. How customers describe the problem, where the product wins and where it struggles, whether the story holds in front of a CFO.
  3. I write it down as I go. Talk track, demo, pilot plan, business case, objections, pricing. One playbook per sector.
  4. We hire against the playbook. New sellers get a sector, a named account list and a playbook that has already worked.
  5. Partners get the same playbook, in a shorter version, with joint account plans.

Pilot rules, from my own deals

Written success criteria before any pilot starts.
A named person who signs if we hit them.
Procurement, legal, IT security and finance in the loop from week one.
Map the real buying committee in person, beyond LinkedIn.

At Kotak, putting everything in writing and keeping everyone in the loop is how a vendor with no ISO certificate got through a bank's onboarding. I have also lost a deal because the real deciders never showed up on our account map. Both lessons are in these four rules.
