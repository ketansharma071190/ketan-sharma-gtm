India built the mule score. Each bank still owns what happens after it.

This is how I would open India for Quantexa from your Singapore hub. I built it from public sources and from two years selling AI into Indian banks and insurers. Where I am guessing, I say so.

One scam payment, step by stepfrom 1 January 2027
  1. The payment customer's bank
    A customer is pushed into paying, often by a fake officer on a video call. From 1 January 2027 a payment made by granting approval under coercion or duress counts as fraud, and the bank has to prove the customer was negligent before it refuses to pay back.
  2. The money lands receiving bank
    It reaches a mule account at another bank. RBI's MuleHunter.AI gives that account a probability score. It was live in 26 banks on 31 March 2026, with 4 more underway. For small refunds, this receiving bank now pays a share.
  3. After the score Quantexa's step
    RBI's own report says what happens next is decided by each bank's internal assessment and due diligence. That is where one flagged account has to be linked to the customers, devices, addresses and payments around it. It is the kind of linking Quantexa already does in financial crime for Standard Chartered and HSBC.
  4. The refund claim both banks
    To refuse a claim, the bank needs evidence of negligence. A clear picture of the mule ring helps it decide fast, pay where it must, and defend a refusal where it can. my read
Sources: RBI Commercial Banks Third Amendment Directions, 24 Jun 2026 · RBI Annual Report 2025-26, ch. VI, para VI.57
Why India, why now

The clock is already running

Every figure is dated and sourced. Rupee figures stay in rupees: 1 crore is 10 million, 1 lakh is 100,000.

  1. Calendar 2025

    Indians reported losing Rs 22,495 crore to cyber fraud, most of it to investment scams.

    MHA data, as reported by ThePrint
  2. FY 2025-26

    Banks reported 10,114 frauds of Rs 1 lakh or more, worth Rs 48,021 crore, against 23,722 worth Rs 32,803 crore the year before. Rs 30,199 crore of that is 314 older cases re-reported after a Supreme Court ruling. Loans carry 85% of the amount, and public sector banks 74.5%.

    RBI Annual Report 2025-26, Tables VI.2 and VI.3 with their notes
  3. 31 Mar 2026

    MuleHunter.AI is live in 26 banks, with 4 more underway. RBI's Digital Payment Intelligence Platform has 7 banks in its first pilot phase: a shared registry of reported fraud, with real-time risk scores planned for phase two.

    RBI Annual Report 2025-26, paras VI.57 and VI.66
  4. 24 Jun 2026

    RBI issues amendment directions for commercial banks and six other kinds of bank, replacing the old rules on customer liability in digital transactions.

    RBI press release 2026-2027/533
  5. 1 Jan 2027

    The rules apply. Coerced payments count as fraudulent. The burden of proving customer liability sits with the bank. Zero liability for third-party breaches reported within 5 calendar days.

    RBI/2026-27/167, paras 4(15A), 76K and 76M
  6. Through 2027

    For one year, a person who loses up to Rs 50,000 and is found careless can still get 85% back, capped at Rs 25,000, once in a lifetime, if they report to the 1930 helpline or the national cybercrime portal and to the bank within 5 days. RBI pays 65%. The customer's bank pays 10%. The bank where the money first landed pays 10%.

    RBI/2026-27/167, paras 76N, 76T and 76U

First 90 days

Turn one pilot into a reference

What I would do, and what you would see at the end of each month.

What I do

  • Learn the platform and the Standard Chartered story with your Singapore team.
  • Get three answers on paper: whether the Standard Chartered and HSBC contracts reach India, how we host for an Indian bank, and which partner fronts public sector tenders.
  • Map the 10 banks' buying committees, in person in Mumbai.
  • Hold 15 first meetings with fraud and AML heads.

What you see by day 30

  • Ten account maps with named people and who really signs.
  • The India scope answer for your two global banks.
  • One page on which two banks go first, and why.

What I do

  • Write pilot plans with the first two banks: linked mule accounts found beyond the flags, time to close a case, false positives, and who signs if we hit them.
  • Bring procurement, legal, IT security and finance in from the first week.
  • Agree terms with one partner for public sector access.
  • Keep the next four banks warm with what we are learning.

What you see by day 60

  • Two pilots agreed in writing with a named signer, or a clear no and the reason.
  • A weekly one-page note: meetings, stage, next step, by bank.
  • Partner terms ready to sign.

What I do

  • Run the first pilot on an agreed slice of data, with a review every two weeks.
  • Write the result up with the bank's sign-off so the next bank can read it.
  • Take it to the receiving-side banks and the first public sector bank.
  • Write the 12-month India plan: revenue, cost, hires, and when India pays its way.

What you see by day 90

  • Early numbers from one Indian bank that the next one will believe.
  • A pipeline you can read by group: your banks, private, public.
  • A clear call on whether India earns a second hire.

No Indian bank signs a platform licence inside 90 days. The honest day-90 result is a pilot running against written goals, a named signer, and early numbers we can show the next bank.
