Buyer map

Who buys, who signs, bank by bank.

RBI's fraud rules make an official of General Manager rank or above own fraud monitoring, and every bank puts that person somewhere different.

How a pilot gets signed

BuysFraud head

Titles vary: Head of Fraud Risk Management, Group Head Fraud Control, Group Head Financial Crime, Chief of Vigilance.

Opens the door, signs above the limitCRO

The CRO meeting opens the door; the fraud head buys. The CRO signs when the spend is above the fraud head's delegated limit, or it opens a new risk category.

Backs itChief Compliance Officer, or the AML officer

Owns the new KYC rule on mule accounts, the debit holds, and the reports to India's Financial Intelligence Unit. A list that names mule accounts early helps all three.

Clears itCISO, and the data protection officer

First question: how does the file get into the bank? A bank-hosted secure file transfer, or a single sign-on portal open only to the bank's IP addresses. The CTO or CDO joins only if Apate feeds a live fraud engine.

Uses itThe fraud control unit

Analysts check each listed account against the bank's books every morning, and hold or watch it.

Sees resultsSCBMF and the CFO

The board's fraud committee sees outcomes. The CFO sets the yearly envelope in January to March.

Our read

A feed where customer records stay inside the bank, and what comes back is the accounts the scammers named, probably sits outside "material outsourcing" under Para 53(2) of RBI's 2025 outsourcing rules. That keeps the pilot inside the fraud head's own authority, and it is the fastest path to a signature.
