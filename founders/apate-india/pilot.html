Pilot playbook

Eight weeks of lists, no integration, four numbers.

Built so the fraud head can say yes without an IT project, the CISO can clear it in weeks, and the readout turns straight into a signature.

At a glance

Length
Eight weeks of lists. For the warm bank, 4 January to 26 February, with the readout on 5 March. The usual window is 8 to 12 weeks; this bank is already trialling, so eight is enough.
Where the scams come from
Each day the bank's fraud team passes Apate the scammer numbers, WhatsApp IDs and UPI handles its customers report. Apate's agents contact each one as a new victim. Apate's own seeded numbers add volume.
What Apate engages
Scams that fake the bank's name or target its customers: digital arrest, fake KYC updates, task scams.
What the bank gets
A daily list of mule accounts, UPI IDs and phone numbers, each with the time Apate first saw it.
Integration
None. One file a day, over a bank-hosted secure file transfer, or a single sign-on portal open only to the bank's IP addresses.
Customer data
None, in either direction. The bank sends only scammer details.
Owner
The fraud head.
Gate
A CISO review of the file channel and the security pack.
Price
Paid and fixed, credited to year one when the bank signs. Apate sets the number.

How the data moves

The dated path, for the warm bank

The contract papers move while the lists run, so the readout only has to trigger a signature. Banks met in December take about three months of scoping and security first, so their pilots start in March.

The four numbers

Each bar is a rate, agreed in writing before the lists start. A count would turn on how many mule accounts happen to sit at one bank. The bars below are our starting proposal, set finally with the fraud head.

  • NewOf the listed accounts at the bank, the share its own tools, MuleHunter.AI included, had not flagged when the list arrived.

    BarHalf or more.

    Measured against the bank's flag log, by timestamp.

  • EarlyDays between Apate first seeing an account and the first complaint against it on 1930 or the national portal.

    BarA median lead of two days or more.

    Measured from the bank's complaint data.

  • HeldOf the new accounts at the bank, the share held, and the rupees held on them.

    BarEight in ten held within a day of the list.

    Measured from the bank's hold records.

The business case, for the CRO and the CFO

Three parts: what one mule case can now cost, what a year of mule accounts can cost the bank, and what the board's fraud committee now asks for.

What the board now asks

Since July the SCBMF, the board's fraud committee, oversees how well fraud risk management works, and RBI wants early warning on mule accounts in near real time. The readout gives the fraud head the number that meeting wants: mule accounts found before the first complaint.

The pilot fee fits inside the fraud head's own budget. The annual contract needs a line in FY28, and banks write those lines between January and March. The CFO sets that envelope and never sees the pilot.

What they will say, and the answer

First draft.We will build on this together, starting from the objections your team already logs in Australia and the US.

  • "We already have MuleHunter.AI."

    MuleHunter learns from how money moves through accounts. Apate names accounts from the scammer's side, often before money lands. The first number counts only accounts MuleHunter had not flagged, which measures the overlap.

  • "We built our own engine."

    Your engine sees your transactions. Apate sees the scammer. The list is one more input to the engine you built.

  • "I4C already sends us the Suspect Registry."

    Registry entries come from complaints and from banks' own flags, after money moved. Apate's accounts come before the first complaint, and the second number measures that lead time.

  • "Where do the scam conversations come from?"

    From you, on day one: the scammer numbers, WhatsApp IDs and UPI handles your customers already report. Apate's agents contact each one as a new victim. Apate's seeded numbers add volume, and the telco route through Mobileum comes later.

  • "Only a few hits will be at our bank."

    About 1 in 75 captured accounts sits at any one large bank, so the bars are rates, not counts. The accounts at other banks still count: your team reports them through the national portal, and that is the fourth number.

  • "Does any data leave India?"

    No customer data moves in either direction. The bank sends only scammer details, and Apate's data sits in an Indian cloud region, with logs kept in India.

  • "Is talking to scammers legal here?"

    The agents talk to scammers on Apate's own numbers, including the scammer details the bank passes on. No customer call is diverted or recorded in the bank pilot. Telco diversion waits for a written legal opinion.

  • "Is this material outsourcing?"

    Our read: no customer data in either direction keeps it outside "material" under Para 53(2). Your compliance team confirms it in the scoping week.

  • "Why should we pay for a pilot?"

    A fee puts a named owner and written bars on it. It is credited to year one when you sign.

  • "Our customers are hit in Tamil, Bengali, Marathi."

    Hindi and English first, then your main languages in the order of your complaint volume.

  • "What happens after eight weeks?"

    A readout against the bars you signed, and the order form the same day. The contract papers are agreed while the lists run.
