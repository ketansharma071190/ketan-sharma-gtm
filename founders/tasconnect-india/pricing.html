What is public

  • A subscription plus a per-transaction charge, paid by the bank, the corporate, or both. SMEs sign up free.
  • Rate cards are quoted privately, deal by deal.
  • At the global peers (C2FO, Taulia, PrimeRevenue) the platform is free to suppliers.

Indian benchmarks, in basis points

  • Bank-side platforms charge banks 3 to 8 bps of disbursals, about 7 on average. Some large banks prefer a one-time licence.
  • A large public bank's white-label tender priced a licence, customisation, and annual support capped at 20% of the licence a year.
  • TReDS charges registration of a few thousand rupees to Rs 50,000 a year, plus 25 to 30 bps a year on transactions.
  • In the brief, discounting alone breaks even at about 12.7 bps of what is paid early. The whole case, with the bank-funded terms and the interest avoided, breaks even at about 55 bps.

The value ceiling, checked

In the 2,000-supplier brief the corporate keeps about Rs 8.9 crore a year in the strict view, before any fee: Rs 1.2 crore from discounting on its own cash, Rs 7.1 crore from longer terms its banks fund, and Rs 0.6 crore of late-payment interest avoided. Against today's liquid fund it keeps Rs 10.1 crore.

So a corporate-paid fee of Rs 1 to 2 crore a year fits either view, with room. Discounting alone carries only Rs 1.2 crore in the strict view, so where the corporate funds with factoring, lead with the bank-funded line and let the banks' volume fee carry more of the price.

Six rules for quoting

  1. Price after scoping.The first meeting is about value; the price follows once the CFO has seen it on his own numbers.
  2. Price on what only a multi-bank platform does.Several banks competing on one screen, own-cash discounting where the corporate earns the discount, one data set for the Ind AS 7 note and the 45-day rule. The price anchors on that value.
  3. Suppliers are free.
  4. Banks pay a volume fee where they fund.Who pays what is decided per program.
  5. Fees stay with the platform.Fees shared back to the buyer push a program toward debt classification. A rival's "information-access fees" to buyers made the Wall Street Journal in 2022.
  6. Multi-year terms, with the expansion entities named in the order form.
