    Where each line comes from

    1 · Discount earned, own cash

    Your cash pays suppliers early and earns the discount.

    return = d / (1 − d) × 365 / days early

    1% for 30 days is 12.3% a year. 0.75% is 9.2%. 0.5% is 6.1%. Only offers above the floor use your cash.

    2 · Days payable, bank-funded

    Banks pay strategic suppliers early at your rating. You pay on the later date.

    cash freed = enrolled spend × extra days / 365

    Non-MSE suppliers only. The supplier comes out level or ahead when the bank's rate beats its own money (worked below). Keep terms near market norms: the Ind AS 7 note shows them either way.

    3 · Compliance and controls

    Small suppliers paid inside 45 days, and one source for the note.

    Late MSE dues carry interest at 3 times the bank rate, compounded monthly: 17.25% nominal at a 5.75% bank rate, about 18.7% effective, and disallowed as a tax deduction. 43B(h) moves the deduction to the year of payment. Credit notes offset automatically.

    Also · Supplier cost of funds

    The supplier swaps its own borrowing rate for yours, or takes a discount it chooses.

    Supplier resilience and, over time, better prices. Price gains sit outside the total, as upside.

      What to quote in a CFO room

      • Quote the India electronics case: about 8 to 10% a year on surplus cash, suppliers' receivable cycle about 25 days shorter, 10,000+ open invoices a day.
      • Explain why the brief shows 10.9%, above that band: this cash costs 8.93%, so only the higher offers use it.
      • Lead with the total, then the line that fits the account: line 1 for cash-rich, line 2 for multi-bank, line 3 for a late MSMED table.
