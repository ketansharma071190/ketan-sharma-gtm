Why an SDR is the wrong first hire here, specifically

01 · The room

Your buyer is an ML or robotics lead who can tell in one line whether the sender understands occlusion, hand tracking or why training-ready data costs six to seven times raw capture. Two years of SDR experience cannot fake that, and in this audience faking it is worse than silence.

02 · The thing to work from

An SDR needs a system: who to contact, what is true, what has worked, what to say when they push back. That system currently lives in your head. Hiring someone to execute a system that has not been written down is how you conclude, wrongly, that outbound does not work.

03 · The order

Build the motion, then buy the volume. An SDR hired after the system exists ramps in weeks against a known playbook. An SDR hired before it exists spends six months inventing one, badly, while you keep selling anyway.

On not liking what AI writes

You are right, and I am not going to argue you out of it. Almost everything AI writes for outbound is bad, and for your market it is worse than bad, because a lab lead can smell a generated email in one line and you have a finite number of them.

So to be exact about where I would use it: not for writing. For research depth per account. The bottleneck in a small, technical market is never typing speed. It is knowing enough about each lab to earn the reply. That is reading papers, release notes, job posts, talks and GitHub activity across a few hundred organisations, which is slow for a human and fast for a machine. The sentence still gets written by someone who understands the buyer.

What you have seen, and rightly dislike

Hi [Name], I came across your work in robotics and wanted to reach out. We help companies like yours accelerate AI training with high quality annotated data. Would you be open to a quick 15 minute chat to explore synergies?

What research depth produces instead

Saw the manipulation demo you posted last month, the bimanual folding one. The failure cases in the last twenty seconds all look like occlusion during the handoff, which is the exact thing dense action labels are for and the exact thing raw capture will not give you.

We turn egocentric capture into training-ready sets: dense action labels, 2D to 3D hand tracking, human verified. Six to seven times the cost of raw capture and it is the difference between footage and a dataset.

If occlusion during contact is not your bottleneck right now, say so and I will leave it. If it is, I will send one annotated clip from your own footage.

The second one is not better writing. It is better knowing. That is the part a system can carry, and it is the part an SDR with two years of experience cannot.
