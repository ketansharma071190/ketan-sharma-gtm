Start India the way Apate works at home: one bank and one telco.

Dali, this is how I would start India for Apate without taking anything away from the US. I built it from public sources and from my years selling AI into Indian banks, so it is a draft. Where I am guessing, I say so.

Where each tool acts in an Indian scammy map, from public sources
  1. The hook call, SMS, WhatsAppTelco spam filters, caller name display, Truecaller. They block or warn.
  2. The conversation hours or daysApate. An agent plays the victim and keeps the scammer busy.
  3. Where to pay mule account, UPI ID, walletApate. The details are captured before anyone pays.
  4. The paymentBank checks pass, because the victim approves the payment with their own PIN or OTP. Behaviour tools such as BioCatch may flag a coached session.
  5. The money landsMuleHunter.AI and the bank's own monitoring watch the receiving account.
  6. The complaintThe NCRP portal, I4C's Suspect Registry, and from 1 January 2027 the bank's refund case.
From 1 January the last step costs the bank more: payments a customer was coerced into now count as fraud, and the bank has to prove the customer was negligent before it refuses to pay back. Steps 2 and 3 are where that cost can be cut, and nobody I found in India works there today. That comes from searching, so treat it as a lead.

What that tells me

  1. India should copy what already works in Australia instead of inventing a new motion: one bank that uses the intelligence and one telco that diverts calls.
  2. India should not take people or money from the US push. It can start lean, with one person on the ground here and the product team in Sydney. my read
  3. Investment fraud is three quarters of the money Indians lose, and much of it recruits through WhatsApp and Telegram groups. That points at Apate Text first, with Voice when a telco is ready. guess on the channel mix
  4. Indian buyers will compare you with what they already have: MuleHunter.AI, the Department of Telecom's fraud risk scores, telco spam filters. The pitch has to show the step where you act and they do not, which is before the money moves.
  5. "Where does the data sit?" will be the first question from every bank and telco. It belongs on page one of every proposal.
Why India, why now

The clock is already running

  1. Calendar 2025

    Indians lost Rs 22,495 crore to cyber fraud across 28.15 lakh complaints. 76 percent of the money went to investment fraud. At about Rs 88 to the dollar that is roughly US$2.5 billion.

    ELP law-firm alert, Jun 2026
  5. 18 Sep 2026

    TRAI notifies rules that make telcos act on AI-flagged spam without waiting for complaints. Flagged call details go to the caller's telco within 2 hours. The rules phase in over the following one to three months.

    Upstox · Free Press Journal · Fox Mandal
  6. 1 Nov 2026

    The Department of Telecom has ordered Airtel, Jio, Vi and BSNL to show the caller's registered name (CNAP) from this date.

    Inc42
  7. 1 Jan 2027

    RBI's revised rules apply. Payments a customer makes "under coercion, intimidation or duress" now count as fraudulent transactions, and the bank must prove customer negligence before it refuses to pay back. Zero liability where the bank was negligent, or for third-party breaches reported within 5 calendar days. For one year, a victim of a small fraud, up to Rs 50,000, can get 85 percent back once, capped at Rs 25,000, shared by RBI, the customer's bank and the bank that received the money.

    ELP law-firm note on the RBI Third Amendment Directions, Jun 2026
  8. About May 2027

    Most obligations under India's data protection rules (DPDP) apply, including the rules on sending data abroad.

    AZB Partners · SCC Online

From January the bank pays for more of the scams it could not see coming. Your agents are inside those scams before the money moves.

I would not oversell this. Banks already had to prove customer fault on unauthorised payments. What is new is that coerced payments now count too, and the payout scheme is small and lasts one year. Nobody knows yet how banks will argue negligence on large digital-arrest losses.

First 90 days

Turn one pilot into a reference

What I would do, and what you would see at the end of each month.

What I do

  • Learn the product with your Sydney team, and test the agents in Hindi and Hinglish on real scam scripts.
  • Get two answers on paper: India hosting, and the legal view on diverting and recording calls.
  • Build a list of 30 named accounts: large banks, mid-sized banks, two telcos, two UPI apps.
  • Hold 15 first meetings with fraud heads, most of them in person in Mumbai.
  • Open the RBI Innovation Hub and I4C conversations.

What you see by day 30

  • One bank and one telco chosen, with the reasons on one page.
  • The hosting and legal answers.
  • Notes from 15 meetings: what Indian buyers say, in their words.

What I do

  • Write the pilot plan with the bank: success criteria, timeline, who signs if we hit them.
  • Take it through procurement, legal, IT security and finance from the first week.
  • Start the bank pilot on Apate Text. Start the telco pilot if the legal answer allows it.
  • Keep the next three banks warm with what we are learning.

What you see by day 60

  • A pilot agreed in writing with a named signer, or a clear no and the reason.
  • A short weekly note: accounts found, accounts the bank had not seen.
  • A pipeline you can read by bank, telco and UPI app.

What I do

  • Run the pilot against its written goals, with a review every two weeks.
  • Write the result up with the bank's sign-off, so we can show it to the next bank.
  • Take it to the mid-sized banks and to the second telco.
  • Write the 12-month India plan: revenue, cost, hiring, and when India pays its way.

What you see by day 90

  • Early numbers from one Indian bank that the next bank will believe.
  • Two or three follow-on conversations using them.
  • A clear call on whether India earns a second person.

A big Indian bank will not sign a year's contract inside 90 days. The realistic day-90 result is a pilot running against written goals, a named signer, and early numbers we can show the next bank. That is how we did it at Simplifai.
