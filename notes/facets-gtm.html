A read on Facets, from the outside

Three doors, and no one who owns all three

Written for Pravanjan and the Facets founding team by Ketan Sharma, a B2B sales leader in Pune. Everything below comes from your own public pages, each with its source, checked on 27 September 2026.

What I saw

1. The pricing page opens three separate buying doors

A $799 a month pay-as-you-go plan with a 14-day free trial, a custom Enterprise contract that can be self-hosted in the buyer's VPC, and transactable listings on the AWS, Azure and Google Cloud marketplaces. Each door is a different sale: trial conversion, a security-reviewed enterprise cycle, and cloud co-sell with a hyperscaler's own reps.

Source: facets.cloud/pricing

2. The team page lists the sellers but no one who owns the number

Under "Everyone building Facets" the commercial side is a Head of Growth and Strategy, a Founder's Office for Sales, a Founder's Office for GTM, one Enterprise Account Executive and one Senior SDR. There is no sales leader on the page. That is a founder-led motion with good people plugged into it, and it is now being asked to run all three doors above.

Source: facets.cloud/company

3. The pitch has moved ahead of the proof

The homepage now leads with AI: Facets "gives AI coding agents like Claude Code access to production-ready infrastructure." The customer results page, which is strong, proves the earlier story: about 80% less DevOps toil, 8 to 25 times faster shipping, with Capillary, Niyo, MPL, Purplle and Aster named. The largest proof point, a Fortune 50 automaker with 1,500 engineers, is anonymous. So the new buyer, a team adopting coding agents, does not yet have a reference that looks like them.

Sources: facets.cloud, facets.cloud/customers

Why it matters for revenue

Three doors with no single owner usually means the enterprise door gets the founders' time, the trial door leaks quietly, and the marketplace door stays a listing rather than a pipeline source. And SDRs sent out with the agent story but only platform-engineering references will book meetings that stall at "who else like us uses this?"

What I would do in the first 30 days

  1. Week 1. Sit in on every live deal and demo, and pull every trial sign-up from the last two quarters. Map which door each rupee and dollar of pipeline actually came through.
  2. Week 2. Write one page of qualification per door: who buys, what triggers it, what kills it. Register the top enterprise deals for co-sell on the marketplace where most of them already spend.
  3. Week 3. Go back to two or three of the named customers and turn them into agent-era references: are their developers already using coding agents on Facets? One named story beats ten anonymous ones.
  4. Week 4. A pipeline target per door, a simple weekly forecast the founders can trust, and a written hiring plan for the next seller only after the door it serves has proved it can fill.

Where I could be wrong

The team page may lag reality and a sales leader may already be in place. Founder-led selling may be a deliberate choice until a revenue line is crossed. And I have no view of your pipeline, so the split across the three doors is a question, not a claim.

Ketan Sharma. Built the commercial function at ZenAdmin and led APAC sales at Simplifai. Happy to be told what I got wrong over 15 minutes.
