The rule that sets the order. Under IRDAI's wellness framework, first set out in its circular of 4 Sep 2020 (IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/233/09/2020), insurers can reward wellness with renewal discounts, but the feature and its reward method have to be filed with the product and offered alike to similarly placed members. So 150 either rides a feature an insurer has already filed, or sits outside the policy as an employer-paid benefit that a broker places. The second route is open today, and it is where the data comes from.

How the math works, and what is assumed

engaged = offered lives x engaged %. Claims avoided a year = engaged x baseline frequency x drop; cost saved multiplies that by the average claim. Loss-ratio points = cost saved / (covered lives x premium per life). "Claims savings can fund" = frequency x drop x average claim: the most an insurer could pay per engaged member a year out of the claims line alone and break even.

Proof sizes use a two-arm comparison (engaged vs matched non-engaged) at the usual 5% two-sided significance and 80% power. Claims: two-proportion formula on the annual claim rate. HbA1c: difference in means with a standard deviation of 1.0 point assumed.

Every slider default is a planning assumption, not a 150 or insurer figure. For scale: across all Indian health insurance in FY25, about 3.26 crore claims were settled for about Rs 94,248 crore (IRDAI annual report), roughly Rs 29,000 a claim. The high-risk slice is the 26.7% of Indian adults with diabetes (11.4%) or prediabetes (15.3%) in the ICMR-INDIAB study (Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology, 2023), assumed here to claim at twice the book's rate. Insured employees and their families ran higher in Loop's Workforce Health Index 2026, with 38.7% of HbA1c results in the prediabetes or diabetes range. A matched design that uses each member's prior-year claims trims the claims sample, but not by anything like the gap between the two bars.
