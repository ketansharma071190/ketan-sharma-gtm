Call one, scripted

A finance lead at a Series D EdTech company asks for a lower MDR

Your careers page names listed, Series D and Series E customers in EdTech and Travel. source Your August post says a deal is often won before the first demo, and that a request for a lower MDR can hide a success-rate, support or reconciliation problem. source Here is how we would run that call. The buyer's numbers are invented.

Buyer

We like what we have seen. Your card rate is higher than what we pay today. Can you come down?

Us

We can talk about rate. First, of your international orders last month, what share completed?

Buyer

Around 70%, I think.

Us

Then rate is probably the smaller lever. Two checks first. Is that 70% per attempt or per order? Retries inflate failures. And on a typical ticket, what lands in INR after fees and FX today? We will show you the same number with us. That is the number to compare, not the headline rate.

Buyer

Per order, I think. I'd have to check the INR.

Us

Here is the ceiling on $1M a month of international orders.

Cut the rate by 0.5 points on the $700K that clears$3,500 a month saved
Lift success from 70% to 85%up to $150,000 a month more collected

A ceiling, not a promise: some failed orders come back through another payment method. On a real call we would ask for a week of gateway data and size it together. xPay's own PayGlocal comparison runs the same 15-point, $150,000 sum source, the home page claims 95% success source, and the fee schedule lists FX conversion as handled by AD1 banks at bank rates source. The PayGlocal post says live mid-market FX with zero hidden markup source, so we would confirm which applies before quoting a landed-INR number.

Us

Second question: how long does your team spend matching payouts to invoices and chasing FIRCs? With xPay the FIRC is available from the dashboard.

Then their engineer joins

What we ask before we answer anything:

  1. Who processes your international cards today, and would we replace them or sit alongside?
  2. Which three countries cause most of your failures?
  3. When a subscription renewal fails, who retries, and when?
  4. How does finance match a payout to invoices, and what do you need on each FIRC?

Us

So the ask today is not a rate. It is a pilot: your engineer builds against our sandbox, then you send one country's live traffic through us for two weeks and compare success on the same card mix. Five questions a buyer brings to call one

What we would need to answer in one line

Four come off xPay's public pages, one off RBI's. Some you have probably fixed or chosen on purpose.

Question 1 of 5

"Which settlement applies to us?"

Home page: bank-account collections settled in 24 hours. source Terms: INR to the merchant's Indian account once every two weeks, a timeline that "may be adjusted over time based on the procedures imposed by the regulator". source Skydo comparison post: T+1 INR payouts. source Docs: payouts in USD, 24 to 48 working hours, Mondays and Thursdays by default, $500 minimum. source

Who asks: the finance lead planning cash flow.

What would help: if each line belongs to a different product, one table saying which applies to whom settles it before a call.

Question 2 of 5

"Why does this page say 5% a sale?"

A marketplace page, live and listed in your sitemap, quotes "5% /sale" on a Basic plan that bundles xPay Marketing with xPay Payments, behind "Join Waitlist" buttons. source The pricing page says 3% + $0.35. source

Who asks: the buyer who Googles xPay before replying.

How we would answer: "That is the marketplace plan, which bundles marketing with payments. Card checkout on its own is 3% + $0.35." If that plan is retired, a redirect stops the next buyer asking.

Question 3 of 5

"Is there a price between list and enterprise?"

Standard is 3% + $0.35. The next tier is Enterprise, above $750K a month processed internationally, with volume-based tiered pricing. source

Who asks: a company doing $200K to $700K a month, comparing list price with its current rate without ever calling you.

What would help: if the gap is there to push that company to Talk to The Team, one line on where volume pricing starts makes the push work.

Question 4 of 5

"Can you show us a customer like us?"

Testimonials from PeerUp, Radical Health, Attikkel Technologies, Remedo.io and Edureka source. The strongest story we saw is Aniket's mid-May post: a large Indian consumer company whose success rate went "up by ~12% as compared to the incumbent", which he put at "an extra $5M in Annualized revenue". source

Who asks: the champion carrying your proof through the relay your June post describes: finance, sales, product and tech, back to sales, then finance and management. source

What would help: large customers often will not be named. An anonymised one-pager with those numbers travels through that relay better than a post.

Question 5 of 5

"What changes for us on 1 Oct?"

RBI's new FEMA export regulations take effect on 1 Oct 2026. For services other than software, the new work is an EDF declaration within 30 days of the end of the month each invoice is raised (they may also file by the day payment arrives); software's SOFTEX moves to the same EDF. The time to realise stays at nine months from the invoice date: a 22 Sep amendment replaced the fifteen months in the January text before it took effect. Rupee-invoiced exports get twelve months. regulations current rule SOFTEX change

Who asks: the finance lead at a services exporter that invoices clients abroad every month and gets paid by wire.

What would help: it is a dated reason for that finance lead to review how export receipts are collected and documented this quarter. For a client who pays by wire, one answer on the collection side is a USD virtual bank account on the invoice, taking ACH, domestic wire or SWIFT. source That is a conversation xPay can open.
