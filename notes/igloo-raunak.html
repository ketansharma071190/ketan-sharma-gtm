When each play has to start

What runs the desk

Your stack takes a product launch from months to days. What is left is the partner's clock, and moving that is a sales job. This is the machine any partner manager on the team would run, not a one-person show.

01

Dated by their calendar

Every partner row is dated by the moment its users buy. The start-by date falls out of the clock, so nobody asks when we can launch; the desk says when we must start.

02

Two owners, one date

Every signature carries a first-policy date and two named owners, theirs and ours, on page one of the MOU. A signed partner without both is not counted as won.

03

A launch kit per market

Built once, reused on every account: vendor-risk answers for that market's rules, a one-page term sheet with three commercial models, products already approved with insurers, a sandbox journey in the partner's colours on day one. That is what lets the gates run side by side.

04

Monday, then the QBR

Monday covers every row with a start-by date inside 30 days and every gate that slipped. Each QBR opens with the partner's next moment and ends in a decision: the slot, the offer, the attach target. Not an activity recap.
