AI is as good as the person behind it

I say this because I watched it happen. The second thing I run this way is my own job search. About 1,590 automated job alerts got me one conversation. Leads I spotted myself on LinkedIn started 10 of about 21. So I had the agents search the way I search, hiring posts from the last 24 hours, and cut the rest.

So far it has judged 967 roles. For 402 the answer was don't apply, find the person. For 135 it was skip. Everyone sits in one CRM, and 1,033 of 1,067 logged actions have proof attached. Nothing goes to anybody until a check passes: not an ex-colleague, not on hold, not touched last week. It has stopped 321.

About 50 billion tokens across Claude and Codex since March, most of it cache. Nothing next to Cars24's 1.3 trillion a month, but this is one person. I don't write the code. Around 14,000 lines of Python written to my rules, six test suites, none failing today.

The part you'd care about is what broke. In early October everything hit one weekly usage limit. For four days every job fired on time and died in two seconds, and the scheduler kept showing a healthy last run. Now I count what came out and ignore the status. Your customers will find this kind of thing a few weeks after go-live, and that's when they decide if they trust you.
