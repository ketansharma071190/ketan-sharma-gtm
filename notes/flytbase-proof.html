What I would own, why it moves the number, and the first piece of it, already built from outside.

1What I would own

Positioning. Not a tagline. Every place a prospect meets us, and making sure sales walks into every conversation already carrying the full range of what we do.

That means the site, the videos, what a rep has in hand before a first call, and what gets sent to a customer's CFO afterwards. One story, told the same way in all of them.

What it moves: average contract value, dramatically, because the ceiling becomes visible from the first minute. And the sales cycle, somewhat, because the first call stops being spent explaining what we are.

2Where we are

We have the strongest evidence base in this category. Twenty four published deployments, twenty industries, six continents. A copper mine in Zambia flown from a control room in another country. A fire brigade in Kiel wired straight into the 112 dispatch line. Australia's first airport BVLOS approval. A solar operator that grew seven times in four years without growing the team.

Sixteen of those twenty four stories already come with the customer on video. The raw material is not the problem.

4Where we get to

A buyer arrives already knowing how far this goes, having half sorted himself. A rep opens a new account and the picture is already built for that prospect. The customer's CFO gets a page that makes the case in his own numbers. ACV rises, the cycle shortens, and none of it depends on which seller picked up the phone.

5The vessel

Cards, not case studies.

A case study is an article, and nobody reads twenty four of them. A card is an object. You collect it, compare it, show it to someone else. A buyer answers three questions and gets the cards that are his, plus the ones one size above him. Each opens into the customer on video, the operation running, the published numbers, and what it returned.

The four questions already on our homepage, BVLOS path, ROI framework, data to intelligence, IT and compliance, become the four things every card answers. Then the same cards run through all three places:

9Three small things, all quick

The case studies page shows zeros to anything that does not scroll.Every headline number is served as 0 and only counts up when a person scrolls. A crawler, a link preview, or a model answering a buyer's question sees "0st BVLOS approval" and "0 USD saved". Our robots.txt invites five AI crawlers in and serves them that.
One tile understates our best number by ten times.The TNB story is titled "90x" and its body says a 90-fold improvement. The stat tile on the same page says 90%.
The platform share card still sells the old category.Its title reads "World's first drone autonomy platform" while the headline on the same page says "The Physical AI platform behind the world's largest drone dock programs."

10Where I could be wrong

  • If deal size is set by the customer's budget rather than the conversation, this is a targeting problem, not a visibility one.
  • If our best sellers already work from something written down that simply is not reaching the others, this is a smaller job than I think.
  • If long first calls come from procurement or regulation rather than from confusion about what we are.
  • If someone already owns this and I could not see it from outside.

Any of those would show within the first month. Everything here comes from our public pages and from what you told me. I have used no revenue or headcount numbers, because the ones visible from outside are guesses.
