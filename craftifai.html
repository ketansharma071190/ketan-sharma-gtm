Three things I think are actually going on

01. The whole business right now is pilot-to-paid, and that is a different sale from the one that won the pilots

A pilot gets approved by an engineering lead who is curious and has a discretionary budget. Converting it to paid needs a different signature, usually procurement plus whoever owns the safety and quality process. The pitch that wins the pilot is "this will save my team months". The pitch that wins the contract is "this will not get us sued". Most seed companies discover this in month nine, after the pilots quietly expire.

02. Your real objection is not price or accuracy. It is liability.

You are generating firmware for drones, cameras, automotive and aerospace. If AI-written firmware fails in the field, someone has to own it. No embedded org has a policy for that yet, so the honest answer from your buyer is not "no", it is "I do not know who signs this". That is not a sales objection you close, it is a proof artefact you build once and reuse: what Orbit generates, what it never touches, how it is verified, and what the customer's own engineers still sign off on.

Whoever writes that document first defines the category. It is also the single highest-leverage thing a GTM person could build for you, and it is not a deck.

03. Indian OEMs and a US-listed semiconductor company are two different companies

An Indian robotics or drone OEM is a fast, price-sensitive, founder-to-founder sale where your PhonePe SmartSpeaker background is genuinely load-bearing. A US-listed semiconductor company is nine to eighteen months, a buying committee, security review, and your India-ness is a question rather than an asset. Both are in your pilot list. At eighteen months old with a $3M seed you can fund one of those motions properly, not two.

Picking one is not narrowing. It is the only way anything compounds.

Where I might be wrong: you may already have paid conversions I cannot see, the US semi relationship may be a design partnership rather than a sales motion, and Yashwant's Xilinx and Sima.ai network may make the US path far warmer than it looks from outside. All three would change the answer.

So, two shapes. Pick the one that fits.
